Homoglyph / Confusable Character Detector

Spot characters that look like others: a domain that reads paypal.com but uses a Cyrillic letter, an impersonating account like 'аdmin', or fullwidth forms. It checks mixed scripts inside a word, shows an ASCII skeleton and punycode, and can compare two strings to tell whether they look identical.

What this tool does

Check whether look-alike characters are mixed into domains, usernames or code. Your text is not uploaded.

  • Detection: Cyrillic and Greek upper / lower case, fullwidth ASCII, mathematical and enclosed forms, and Hangul jamo and ideographs (ㅇ ㅡ ㅣ 一 〇) that resemble ASCII
  • Mixed scripts: warns when one word mixes e.g. Latin and Cyrillic (normal CJK combinations are allowed)
  • Skeleton: the text with look-alikes folded to ASCII and punycode (xn--) for domain-like words
  • Compare: tells you when text B has the same skeleton (possible spoof) and flags ASCII that is ambiguous in some fonts (l / I / 1, O / 0)

💡 Tips

  • A domain your browser shows as xn-- usually mixes scripts; check it before clicking.
  • Check username uniqueness with NFKC normalisation plus skeleton comparison, not raw string equality, to block impersonators.
  • In code review, Cyrillic letters hidden in identifiers can compile but behave differently from what you expect.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does it find every look-alike?

It covers the commonly abused characters, not the full Unicode confusables data, so confirm important decisions with the punycode form too.

Is normal Korean text flagged?

Hangul alone, or Hangul with Latin and Han, is not warned about. Only jamo that resemble ASCII are shown, for reference.

Is my data uploaded to a server?

No. Everything is processed inside your browser; what you enter is never sent to or stored on a server, so you can safely paste data containing personal information, internal settings or API keys.
